Rune Legibility

Sixteen candidate glyphs, measured against the ten already in use — and the two runes that are failing today.

Every number here comes from tools/rune_legibility.py, a faithful port of the game’s own recognizer run against strokes drawn with a hand’s wobble and reported by a digitizer that is losing sample rate and resolution. Nothing is an opinion about how a shape feels to draw.

The same drawing every time. Only the digitizer degrades, the way a hot or nearly-flat phone degrades it.

It holds perfectly down to 24 samples, then collapses into a plain horizontal line — earth. And earth + fury is the earthquake, so on a hot phone asking the earth to convulse plants a wood instead.

What the numbers say

One candidate out of sixteen clears the bar, and only barely. The closed box sits at 67.9 against a threshold of 68 — which is to say it does not really clear it either. Everything else is nearer to a rune already in use than the recognizer’s own tolerance.

That is the finding, not a shortage of ideas. Six of the ten runes in use are smooth curves living in one another’s shape-space — circle, spiral, reverse spiral, wave, arc, caret. A new smooth curve has nowhere to go, and a new sharp shape has to fight the three straight lines. The alphabet is not short of a glyph; it is full.

Corners survive what curvature does not. Every shape here is 100% robust to the thermal degradation once it is the only claimant — the failures above are collisions, not blur. Sharp corners and closure survive a coarse grid; a five-tooth zigzag and a not-quite-closed loop do not.

So the honest options are to thin the curves — move two or three of the six onto corner-and-crossing forms and free the space — or to accept the box for life, take fury onto a two-corner Z, and live with tight margins.

Distances are mean per-point distance between normalised 48-point strokes, the same measure the game uses to pick a rune. Survival percentages are 30–60 drawings per condition with hand wobble. Regenerate with python3 tools/rune_legibility.py.
